The `/debug` oracle should have shown you the trick. Feed it
`?id=1%20UNION%20SELECT%201` and it reports something like:

```
forwarded-to-backend: '1UNIONSELECT1'
contains-0x20-space: False
```

The filter strips every space — and, if you test them, every tab (`%09`), every
`%20`, and every `+` too. Your keywords fuse into one unparseable blob. That is
the entire defence: _no whitespace = no injection_, or so its author believed.

The author's mistake is thinking a space is the **only** way to separate SQL
tokens. It is not. MySQL accepts several separators that contain no `0x20` byte at
all. The most reliable is the **inline comment** `/**/`: anywhere a space is
legal between tokens, an empty `/**/` works just as well, and it is pure
punctuation — nothing for a whitespace filter to catch. (Raw newlines, sent as
`%0a`, and parentheses are two more separators the filter never considered — try
`/debug` with each and watch them survive.)

So take a normal UNION and swap every space for `/**/`. Keep checking `/debug`
until it forwards a valid-looking, space-free statement. Remember two things:
the context is numeric (no quote needed), and the hidden admin is the row where
`id=1`.
