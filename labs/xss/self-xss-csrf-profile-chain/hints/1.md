Your bio is an XSS sink — but only for you. `GET /profile` (your own profile)
renders the bio **raw**, while `GET /u/<name>` (how everyone else sees it) escapes
it. So `<img src=x onerror=...>` in your bio runs only in **your** session. You
also can't just load the admin's `/profile` — it's the owner's own page (403 for
you), and `/u/admin` escapes the bio. This is the "self-XSS, who cares" trap.

The escalation is about **who sets the admin's bio**. Look closely at the
bio-update endpoint: `GET /profile/update?bio=...`. Two things stand out — the HTTP
method, and something a state-changing request normally has but this one doesn't.
If another site could make the admin's browser hit that URL, whose bio changes?
