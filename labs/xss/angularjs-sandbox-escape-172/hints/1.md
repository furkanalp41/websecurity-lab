Request `/?name=<b>test</b>` and view the page source. Your `<b>` tags come back
**escaped** as `&lt;b&gt;` — the server HTML-escapes the reflected name, so
`<script>` and event-handler injection are dead. A classic reflected-XSS payload
will get you nowhere here.

Now look at the wrapper around the greeting: `<div ng-app>`, and the
`<script src="/vendor/lib.min.js">` in the head. That is **AngularJS**. AngularJS
compiles everything inside an `ng-app` region and evaluates any `{{ ... }}`
interpolation it finds — and HTML-escaping does nothing to the `{{ }}` grammar.

Try `/?name={{7*7}}` and look at the rendered page (in a browser, not just the raw
HTML). Do you see `49`? Then your input is being evaluated as an AngularJS
expression, not shown as text. That is the vulnerability class: **client-side
template injection (CSTI)**.

Running an expression in _your own_ browser gets you nothing, though. Whose browser
holds the cookie worth stealing, and what feature lets you hand that person a URL?
